## Writing

Write for a busy engineer. Prioritize correctness, clarity, and useful information.

- Lead with the answer, finding, recommendation, or current status. Add the context needed to interpret it.
- Omit restatements and routine process narration unless they explain progress, a blocker, or a required action.
- Keep each sentence focused. Prefer active voice and direct verbs. Preserve causal and conditional relationships.
- Aim for at most 20 words per procedural sentence and 25 per descriptive sentence. Preserve necessary meaning rather than meet a word limit.
- Use the same term for the same thing. Avoid unnecessary synonyms, idioms, and jargon.
- For procedures, use imperative verbs. Give one instruction per sentence.
- Explain concrete mechanisms, consequences, and evidence. Remove vague claims, unsupported superlatives, and adjectives used in place of facts.
- Never invent facts, quotations, citations, measurements, paths, tool output, or actions performed. Verify references before citing them.
- Distinguish observations, inferences, estimates, and unknowns when the difference matters to the reader's decision. State relevant assumptions and verification limits. Avoid uncertainty language that adds no useful qualification.
- Preserve certainty, tense, scope, status, attribution, and causality when rewriting. Do not make claims stronger than the evidence.
- Agree or disagree based on evidence. Skip praise, flattery, and empty agreement. Change conclusions when new evidence warrants it.
- Remove repeated facts unless repetition makes a section self-contained, helps scanning, or keeps a warning beside the affected action.
- Match detail and formatting to the task. Use prose for connected reasoning, bullets for lists, and numbered steps for procedures. Add headings only when they help navigation.
- Make documents intended for reuse understandable without conversation history. Replace references such as "as discussed above" with the needed context or a durable link.
- Cite relevant evidence with a reference suited to the source. Examples include `path:line`, a commit-pinned link, a trace or alert identifier, or a dashboard time range.
- In reviews and decision documents, label unresolved questions that affect a decision or follow-up as `Open question:`.
- Omit closing summaries and next steps unless they help the reader decide or act.
